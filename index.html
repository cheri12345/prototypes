Lending Lab — UX Change Log

Overall: The foundation is strong. This is a refinement pass focused on clarity, hierarchy, trust and reducing friction — not a major redesign.

Change Why
Apply CHELCIE brand colours + typography Makes Lending Lab feel connected to the CHELCIE ecosystem and less generic/AI-generated.
Remove duplicate “Lending Lab / Powered by CHELCIE” Reduces clutter and gives the value proposition more space.
Use sentence case, not ALL CAPS Improves readability and feels more aligned with CHELCIE’s brand.
Remove “Live” badge “Live” is ambiguous and creates unnecessary visual competition.
Replace “Live” with “Decision in minutes” Communicates a meaningful user benefit rather than a status.
Remove green dot from “Indicative terms” A green status dot suggests something is live/available, which is misleading here.
Link “Powered by CHELCIE” to CHELCIE Gives users a clear route back to the parent brand.
Reduce primary CTAs Repeating “Start Funding” everywhere dilutes its impact.
Hero: keep one strong “Start Funding” CTA under Capital Access Creates a clear relationship between the proposition and the action.
Remove left-side Start Funding button Avoids competing CTAs in the hero.
Navigation: retain Start Funding while scrolling Keeps the action accessible without repeatedly dominating the page.
Small Loans section: make Start Funding a text link This section is primarily for exploring needs/terms; the CTA doesn’t need to compete visually.
Review/remove “See How It Works” hero CTA The How It Works content is immediately below, so the extra CTA may be unnecessary.
Make key metrics more prominent Users should understand the core proof points while scanning the page.
Make Steps 1–4 visually larger Makes the four-step process immediately scannable.
Remove hover states from non-clickable elements Avoids implying interactions that don’t exist.
Remove unnecessary “Loans” subtitles Lets the stronger headline do the work and reduces visual noise.
Align the content cards Small visual refinement for consistency and polish.
Keep the funding simulator Gives users a tangible way to understand their potential funding needs.
Lead with “Does not affect your business credit” Addresses a key concern users may have before starting a funding journey.
Update the CHELCIE logo in the application widget Ensures the application experience feels authentically CHELCIE-branded.
Keep the current application flow for now It achieves the immediate “get it live” goal; deeper flow improvements can come later.
Make Eligibility more prominent Users need to quickly understand whether Lending Lab is relevant to them.
Put Eligibility before Start Funding Prevents users from investing time in an application only to discover they aren’t eligible.
Journey: Eligibility → Start Funding → Application Creates a clearer, more supportive progression into the lending flow.
Keep “Free to check”, “10 minutes to apply” & “Clear terms” Strong reassurance points that reduce perceived risk and friction.
Keep “Capital coordination, minus the friction” Strong, simple articulation of the product proposition.

The key principle

Make the important things easy to find, without making everything visually loud.

We want users to be able to understand → check eligibility → start funding without unnecessary clutter, competing CTAs or misleading interactions.

CHELCIE HQ — UX Change Log

  1. Simplified the top navigation

    Made the navigation more seamless and visually quieter so it doesn’t compete with the hero. The focus should land first on what CHELCIE is and why it matters.

  2. Strengthened the first-viewport hierarchy

    Kept “Trust infrastructure for committed capital / Fund with confidence” as the core proposition, with less competing messaging around it. Designed for people who scan rather than read.

  3. Brought credibility metrics higher

    Moved the 46,479 / 3,608 / 8 metrics into the initial experience so credibility and scale are established immediately, without requiring someone to read deeper into the page.

  4. Elevated the key differentiation

    Moved “Approval is where most platforms stop. It is where CHELCIE starts.” higher because it quickly answers why CHELCIE is different from existing tools.

  5. Reordered the story

    The flow is now: What is CHELCIE? → Proof → Why it’s different → How it works → Why now → What each side gets → CTA. This gives someone a clearer mental model before asking them to take action.

  6. Made the two-sided system clearer

    Kept the funder-first direction, while making the relationship between the funder record and recipient experience more explicit.

  7. CTA hierarchy

    Kept both Request Access / Sign Up and See Funder View for now. The primary action should be made deliberate rather than letting two buttons compete by default.

Overall

I don’t think we have a content problem — we have an opportunity to sharpen the hierarchy and sequencing of the strong content we already have.

I’ve also kept the direction aligned with the CHELCIE brand principles of clarity, calm authority and structured intelligence.

Request Access — UX Change Log

  1. Keep it within one viewport

    Design the intake so the entire experience is visible without scrolling. The goal is to make getting started feel quick and easy.

  2. Two-column structure

    Left: reassurance — reviewed within 24 hours, fast response, privacy, and an option to contact us directly. Right: the absolute minimum intake needed to understand who they are and get them started.

  3. Keep the interaction lightweight

    We should avoid making this feel like an application. The visual experience should communicate: “This is easy — I can do this now.”

  4. Request Access vs Sign Up

    This feels like a strategic/product decision rather than just a UX copy choice: Request Access → more curated / controlled. Sign Up → more open / immediate. I’d want to align on which signal we want CHELCIE to send before locking the CTA.

Focused Onboarding — UX Change Log

  1. Designed the initial document and information intake

    Personalised entry — completing the core information upfront lets the dashboard be personalised for organisations and users from the start.

  2. Kept the experience lightweight

    Only a few items are visible at once. Reduces perceived effort: showing less at a time makes the experience feel more manageable.

  3. Made the progress indicator highly visible

    Sets expectations, giving people a clear sense of how much is involved before they commit to finishing.

  4. Framed the journey around 3 core things to complete

    Sets expectations upfront and protects momentum — avoids overwhelming people before they’ve even entered the product.

  5. Used generous negative space and CHELCIE’s editorial direction

    Calm by design — negative space supports busy users and makes the experience feel less administrative.

One thing I’m questioning

Do we actually need all of this information upfront?

There may be an opportunity to ask for the absolute minimum, get users into CHELCIE faster, and collect additional information contextually once they’re already in the product.

Key trade-off: more context upfront means a more complete setup, versus less upfront meaning faster entry and stronger momentum.
